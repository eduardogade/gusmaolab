**Cancer Immunotherapy: Definition and State of the Art**

**1. Defining Cancer Immunotherapy**
Cancer immunotherapy is a therapeutic paradigm that harnesses, enhances, or modifies a patient's own immune system to recognize and eliminate malignant cells, rather than directly targeting the tumor with cytotoxic agents. This approach is fundamentally distinct from traditional chemotherapy and radiotherapy because it modulates the immune microenvironment and can produce durable, "living-drug" responses. The principal classifications of immunotherapy include **immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)**, **adoptive cell transfer** (e.g., CAR-T cells, TCR-T cells, TILs), **cancer vaccines**, **oncolytic viruses**, **cytokines**, and **bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTEs)**. Mechanistically, ICIs release the "brakes" on exhausted T cells, adoptive transfer engineers and expands tumor-reactive lymphocytes ex vivo, vaccines stimulate antigen-specific immunity, and oncolytic viruses mediate direct tumor destruction while eliciting systemic antitumor immune responses.

**2. State of the Art in Checkpoint Blockade, Vaccines, and AI-Driven Personalization**
First-generation ICIs targeting CTLA-4, PD-1, and PD-L1 remain the backbone of standard care, but the frontier has shifted toward **next-generation checkpoints** (LAG-3, TIGIT, TIM-3) and **bispecific antibodies** designed to block two pathways simultaneously. While monotherapy against LAG-3, TIGIT, and TIM-3 has suffered high-profile Phase III setbacks (e.g., RELATIVITY-098, SKYSCRAPER-02, COSTAR Lung), bispecific constructs such as rilvegostomig (PD-1 × TIGIT) are showing encouraging early efficacy in metastatic NSCLC. A parallel SOTA breakthrough is the integration of ICIs with **personalized mRNA neoantigen vaccines**. In the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial, adding intismeran autogene to adjuvant pembrolizumab in resected stage IIB–IV melanoma reduced the risk of recurrence or death by **42%** (HR 0.58) versus pembrolizumab alone, with a 24-month recurrence-free survival rate of 82.2% versus 70%. Complementing these clinical advances, **AI-driven multi-omics** platforms are now being used to predict optimal chemo-immunotherapy combinations, classify tumors into immunotherapy-sensitive subtypes, and guide real-time treatment adaptation by integrating genomic, transcriptomic, and tumor-microenvironment features.

**3. State of the Art in Engineered Cell Therapies and Solid-Tumor Breakthroughs**
CAR-T therapy has transformed hematologic malignancies, but solid tumors remain the field's defining challenge due to **antigen heterogeneity**, an **immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME)**, poor T-cell infiltration, and on-target/off-tumor toxicity. The SOTA response is **"armored" CAR-T cells** engineered to secrete immune-stimulating cytokines—such as IL-12 or IL-18—locally upon tumor engagement, thereby reprogramming the TME and restoring antitumor immunity. Clinical proof-of-concept has been achieved with **IL-15-armored GPC3 CAR-T cells**: in a first-in-human study, IL-15 co-expression significantly increased CAR-T expansion and produced a **33% antitumor response rate** and **66% disease control rate** in solid cancers, with cytokine release syndrome rapidly controlled by an **inducible caspase-9 safety switch**. Beyond CAR-T, **bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTEs)** have crossed the hematology–solid-tumor divide, with FDA approvals of tebentafusp (uveal melanoma) and tarlatamab (small cell lung cancer) establishing proof of concept in solid malignancies. The next frontier is **logic-gated CAR designs** such as the **Tmod™ system**, which combines an activating CAR against a tumor antigen (e.g., mesothelin) with an inhibitory receptor recognizing HLA-A*02 on normal cells; this AND-NOT logic enables selective tumor killing while sparing healthy tissue, and is now entering Phase 1/2 trials (EVEREST-2) with an inducible membrane-tethered IL-12 booster to enhance potency without systemic toxicity.
